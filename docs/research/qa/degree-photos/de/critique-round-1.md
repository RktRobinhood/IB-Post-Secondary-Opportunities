## Score: 6/10

About two thirds of the set is strong and does what the brief asks: the discipline
being done, legible at card size. But ten cards break a hard rule: legible text that
fights the title, a brand mascot or logo, event photography, a child in frame, a
generic people-at-a-computer shot, or a look-alike. Ten is too many to accept. If the
ten below are pulled or replaced with photos as good as the strong core, the set is an
8 or better.

**Strong (keep as they are):** 1 well plate, 2 bioreactors, 3 EEG cap, 7 oscilloscope
Lissajous, 9 total station, 10 solar crew, 11 rotary evaporator, 15 grassland sampler,

## Keep with reservations

- **4 computer & IT**: posed and face-forward, with a vendor badge on the unit. Acceptable because it is the only hardware/networking card, but it is the weakest of the Constructor computing set.
- **5 IR, politics & history**: a 1919 naval tonnage table in an archive. The typed text is small enough under the veil, but the frame is beige and low-contrast. If 35 comes back as another archive or document shot, one of the two must change.
- **8 robotics**: the only photo tied to the institution (the Jacobs/Constructor rover), which is a plus. But the hall is dark and the rover is small at card size. A tighter crop on the rover and arm would help.
- **13 sustainable agriculture**: good hands-in-soil subject, but the cowboy hat and pickup read as a Texas ranch. That is borderline misleading for a degree on the Lower Rhine.
- **24 nature-inspired materials**: a tidy tensile tester on a bench, but static and generic. Nothing says "nature-inspired". Any materials degree could use it.
- **26 sustainable engineering for materials & processes**: an empty bench of glassware and red lab jacks. It is the sixth wet-lab card (with 1, 2, 11, 27, 31) and has no hands. Keep only if 31 is replaced with something outside a lab.
- **30 computer science**: the most overused stock image on the web (Unsplash code screen). It is legible and colourful, but a cliché. Once 33 is replaced, the two must not both be screens.

## Overall notes

1. **Text is the recurring failure** (14, 34, and label-led 35). The rule is simple: if
   you can read a word or formula at 480 px, it will fight the title. Check every
   candidate at card width before proposing it.
2. **Too many wet labs.** Six cards are glass-and-gloves benches: 1, 2, 11, 26, 27, 31.
   On Constructor's page, 1, 2 and 11 sit side by side. They differ in object, so they
   can stay, but no further lab bench is allowed in replacements.
3. **Computing at Constructor needs three distinct looks.** CIT (4) is a rack, CS (30) is
   a code screen, SDT (33) needs a replacement. The replacement must not be a screen, a
   rack or a face at a monitor.
4. **Suggested directions for replacements** (candidates, not prescriptions):
   - 6: the same optics table cropped with no people, or another laser or optics bench with hands on a mount.
   - 12: a geologist at an outcrop with hammer and hand lens, or a core laid out in daylight in the field.
   - 14: Göttingen's plaster and string mathematical models (German, physical, textless), or hands with compass and straightedge.
   - 17: hands probing or soldering a sensor board with no visible maker logo.
   - 23: gloved hands handling or condition-checking an artwork, or a curator arranging a hang with neutral works.
   - 31: biomass in the world rather than a lab: wood chips, a hemp or miscanthus harvest, a biorefinery pilot plant.
   - 32: a ranger or guide with a small group on a boardwalk or in a national park, faces small, no event.
   - 33: see note 3. Options include a robot or drone under test, or a data-centre aisle only if 4 changes.
   - 34: a parliament chamber or seminar room in use, with no projected text.
   - 35: the John F. Kennedy Institute library at FU Berlin if Commons has it, or a reading room of American newspapers or microfilm. It must not be another archive box.
5. **Uniqueness across countries:** do not reuse a Galton board, euro note or wax brain
   from the Spain set as a maths or psychology replacement.
6. Light/dark: nothing here breaks under the veil in either theme. 25 and 8 are already
   dark and will read very dark in dark mode; 25 survives on its pattern, 8 needs the
   tighter crop.
