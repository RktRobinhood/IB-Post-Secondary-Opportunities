# Netherlands degree photos: photo editor, round 1, 6/10 (30 Sep 2026)

I judged `contact-sheet-round-1.jpg` (82 photos, batches A and B). I opened 24 stored
files at 720 px (`src/assets/img/programmes/school-nl-*-720.webp`): every flagged card,
every card whose crop note claims text is "not legible", and every card I suspected of
a look-alike. I read all 82 lines in `docs/research/programme-images/schools-nl.jsonl`.
I compared the set with every accepted set: DE, SE, BE, AT, NO and CH.

## Score: 6/10

This is the richest set in the catalogue, and its best third is as good as anything we
have. Dutch and institution-tied frames are what a student will remember:

- 7, LOFAR's Superterp;
- 52, WUR's Dairy Campus;
- 70, the Aalsmeer flower auction;
- 81, Amsterdam's bike garage under the IJ.

The best photographs are 5 (bird in the hand), 12 (aerial silks), 53 (weather balloon),
61 (soap films), 71 (moth sheet at night), 72 (flame tests), 73 (protein crystals) and
75 (levitating magnet).

But 23 of the 82 fail, a rate like Germany's round 1:

- **Ten repeat an accepted card from another country.** The EEG cap, warehouse, painter,
  engine bay, machine tool, laser optics twice, pipetting, breadboard and grand library
  are all already taken.
- **Two crop notes are wrong again.** 34 has a legible safety sign and 38 a legible
  "Property of NIH" stamp, both on cards whose notes say the text is not legible.
- **Groningen's page has three look-alike pairs:** two conservation cards, two optics
  cards and two glassware benches.
- **A cluster of empty or posed frames:** an empty classroom, a meadow, a dish, a grey
  turbine, and a mocap product shot.

**With the 23 rejects below removed, the remaining 59 score 8/10.**

## Directions for replacements (candidates, not prescriptions)

- **Psychology (RUG):** eye-tracking from behind, a reaction-time set-up, or an
  observation room through a one-way mirror.
- **Logistics engineering (Fontys):** an automated sorting conveyor with parcels, or an
  AGV robot fleet. Not racking.
- **Physiotherapy (Fontys):** gait analysis on a treadmill with markers, or hydrotherapy.
- **Fine art (Hanze):** welding a metal sculpture, throwing on a potter's wheel, or
  etching with a press.
- **Automotive engineering (Fontys):** a solar or student race car being built, e.g.
  Eindhoven/Fontys solar racing, if textless.
- **Mechanical engineering (Hanze):** a hydrogen or propulsion test rig. Not a machine
  tool.
- **Chemistry (RUG):** a reaction in colour (a colour-change titration or
  crystallisation). Not a bench.
- **AI (RUG):** a drone or robot navigating a course, or a human-robot handshake. It
  must differ from NAO (43) and the care robot (68).
- **Biomedical engineering (RUG):** a prosthetic hand or exoskeleton on a test bench.
  Not optics.
- **Applied physics (Fontys):** a plasma or vacuum chamber glowing. Not a laser table.
- **Chemical engineering (RUG):** a pilot-plant distillation column in colour, with no
  signs.
- **Game tech (Hanze):** playtesting with a controller, the screen out of focus. Not
  mocap, which is pending in BE and SE.
- **Medical imaging (Fontys):** the same laser-mask subject without the stamp, or
  ultrasound hands on a phantom.
- **Applied chemistry (Fontys):** flow chemistry, or a pilot reactor with an operator.
  Not pipetting.
- **Electrical engineering (Hanze, Fontys):** high-voltage insulator testing, or
  hands winding a coil. No breadboards and no racks.
- **History (RUG):** a reading-room table with a manuscript, hands in gloves, shot
  close. Not conservation, and not a grand library.
- **Sustainable innovation (TU/e):** a technician on top of a turbine nacelle, or
  solar-car or hydrogen work. Close, not a skyline.
- **Theology (Tilburg):** candles or a choir from behind in a church, or an illuminated
  manuscript close-up. Not a library.
- **Land and water management (WUR):** people building or inspecting a dyke, sluice or
  weir, close enough to see hands.
- **Data science (WUR):** field sensors being installed in a crop, or a drone mapping a
  field.
- **Industrial design engineering (THUAS):** hands shaping a foam or clay prototype.
  Not soldering and not a wide lab.
- **Tourism (WUR):** Giethoorn or Kinderdijk from above or a bridge, boats small and no
  readable faces.
