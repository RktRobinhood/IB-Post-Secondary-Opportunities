## Responsible language evidence and complete current scope

The current [institution language policy](https://www.sdmk.dk/english-speaking-applicants/language-requirements)
says:

> Danish National Academy of Music is a Danish-language higher educational institution.

Its rules make Danish the primary teaching language. They allow English in
individual/small-group teaching at the teacher's discretion and in selected
exchange courses. Ordinary international students are expected to acquire
enough Danish to participate. That distinction is affirmative evidence; the
exclusion is not inferred merely from the site's navigation language.

The [English language page linked by programme navigation](https://www.sdmk.dk/en/study/english-speaking-applicants/language-requirements)
states:

> Group teaching is in Danish.

It also describes English solo lessons by agreement and English summaries as
possible accommodations. These pages agree on the relevant distinction:
international access and English individual instruction do not promise a
full degree taught in English. No conflicting English-degree guarantee was
found. No global Danish recognition evidence was downgraded or rewritten.

separates bachelor's, master's and soloist education. Its bachelor section
contains **eight campus entries**: electronic music/sound art, folk music,
church music, classical music in Odense and Esbjerg, musician/music educator,
and rhythmic music in Odense and Esbjerg. This count describes the current
index, not eight English programmes. The institution-wide language policy
applies to ordinary study across this index; there is no identified
first-year English exception. Master's, soloist and exchange provision do not
create additional school-leaver choices.
