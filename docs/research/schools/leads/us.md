## Country: how an IB student applies

Manifest: "Three application environments, not one: the Common App, the University of California
application, and institutions that take only their own" (https://www.commonapp.org/).

**There is no countrywide deadline.** Each college sets its own, but the pattern is shared:

- **Common App** (most private colleges here, plus Michigan, Illinois): one form, opened for the
  2026-27 season (i.e. autumn 2027 entry) on **1 August 2026** (hint). Each college then sets its own
  early and regular deadlines.
- **Early rounds** (~1 or 15 November): Early Decision (binding: ED I / ED II), Early Action
  (non-binding), or Restrictive/Single-Choice Early Action (Harvard, Yale, Stanford: non-binding but
  bars other private early applications). **Regular Decision** ~1–15 January. Decisions: mid-December
  for early, late March / 1 April for regular. **Reply date 1 May** is the US convention (check per
  college).
- **University of California** (Berkeley): its own application, filing period **1–30 November**
  (hint; a UC press release says the window was expanded — check), no early round.
- **Arizona State**: own application with a priority date and rolling admission.
- **Standardised tests**: several of these colleges may require SAT/ACT again (not searched in this
  pass — from general knowledge, so unverified); check each college's testing page. Not an IB matter, but it decides whether
  an IB student can apply at all without one.
- **IB is not an entry-points threshold here.** The IB page at a US college is about **credit /
  placement after admission** (usually HL 5, 6 or 7; SL rarely). Predicted grades go on the school
  report; final IB results are sent in July.
- Aid: several (Harvard, Yale, MIT, Amherst, Bowdoin, Grinnell, Colby) say they are need-blind or
  meet full need for international applicants — hints; check each.
