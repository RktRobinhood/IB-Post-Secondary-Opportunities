# Application systems, routes, deadlines, and calendar design

## Recommendation in one sentence

Treat applying as a **personalised plan assembled from jurisdiction, application system, applicant route, programme, and intake**—not as one deadline attached to a university—and export only dated events whose scope, timezone, source, and verification state are explicit.

## Scope and research method

This note compares representative undergraduate application systems that are relevant to an IB student applying internationally. It uses first-party application-service, government, and institution sources only. It was researched on **22 September 2026**; cycle dates are examples of the system's behaviour, not permanent constants.

The systems differ in consequential ways:

- some are nationally coordinated and issue at most one offer;
- some are provincial or state gateways that hand the student to an institution;
- some allow unordered choices while others delete lower-ranked choices after an offer;
- some publish one fixed cutoff while others have equal-consideration dates, priority dates, rolling review, repeated offer rounds, or deadlines generated by an applicant's own offer;
- portal submission may be only one step: references, signatures, portfolios, tests, transcripts, fee proof, and result releases can have different owners and deadlines.

The product therefore needs to distinguish a **discovery record** (the programme exists), an **eligibility assessment** (the student appears to meet requirements), and an **application plan** (the exact route and tasks for this applicant and cycle).

## Comparison at a glance

| System | Coverage and route | Choice behaviour | Deadline pattern that matters most |
|---|---|---|---|
| UCAS | Shared UK undergraduate service; some course types and part-time study use other routes | Five choices; initial choices are not a preference ranking; firm/insurance decisions come later | Early and main equal-consideration dates, then Extra/Clearing and applicant-specific reply dates |
| OUAC | Central application service for Ontario universities | Unlimited universities, usually at most three programmes per university; order normally does not affect admission | Group A common deadline; Group B, programme, document, and response dates vary |
| ApplyAlberta | Gateway for Alberta's publicly funded institutions, followed by institution application portals | Separate institution applications; no shared ranked list | Institution- and programme-owned deadlines and transcript tasks |
| EducationPlannerBC | Shared account/application service for B.C. public institutions, with exceptions and external routes | Unlimited applications; institution rules determine programmes per application | Institution/programme open and close dates; post-submit work moves to institution |
| Optagelse.dk / KOT | Nationally coordinated Danish undergraduate route, locally assessed | Eight ranked programmes; at most one offer at the highest attainable priority | Applicant-route cutoff, signature pages, local evidence/tests, priority and diploma cutoffs |
| UniversityAdmissions.se | Coordinated Swedish university application, with international and domestic rounds | Eight ranked bachelor's selections; offers capped at 45 credits and lower choices can be deleted | Round, ranking, fee/exemption, document, results, and reply dates |
| CAO | Central route for listed Irish undergraduate courses, with category exceptions | Two independent ranked lists: ten Level 8 and ten Level 7/6 choices | Normal/late windows, restricted courses, HEAR/DARE, Change of Mind, offer rounds |
| Studielink | Official Dutch registration/application portal; institution stages and exceptions remain | No general rank list; special limits and ranking apply to numerus-fixus programmes | 15 January fixed-intake route, 1 May regular route, selection and triggered acceptance |
| Common App | Multi-institution platform, not a national exclusive US route | Colleges are added individually; admission-plan restrictions can interact across applications | College-owned early/regular/rolling plans plus separate material and reply dates |
| UAC | Application and offer service for undergraduate courses in NSW and the ACT | Five preferences for most undergraduate applicants; one offer per round at highest eligible preference | Repeated apply/pay, document, preference-lock, and offer-release dates |

## System profiles

### United Kingdom — UCAS

**Boundary and route.** UCAS is the UK's shared undergraduate admissions service for participating providers. Full-time undergraduate applications normally flow through it, while part-time study is commonly direct and conservatoire or teacher-training routes have distinct rules. Applicants can apply independently or link to a school/college; a school may impose an earlier internal deadline so it can check the application and add the reference. See [UCAS dates and deadlines](https://www.ucas.com/applying/applying-to-university/dates-and-deadlines-for-uni-applications), [filling in the application](https://www.ucas.com/applying/applying-to-university/filling-in-your-ucas-application), and [UCAS reference routes](https://www.ucas.com/advisers/help-and-training/guides-resources-and-training/writing-references/reference-processes-and-terminology).

**Choices and offers.** An applicant can add up to five choices. They are not an admissions preference order. After decisions arrive, the applicant normally chooses a firm choice and may choose an insurance choice; Extra and Clearing cover later scenarios. See [filling in the application](https://www.ucas.com/applying/applying-to-university/filling-in-your-ucas-application), [firm and insurance decisions](https://www.ucas.com/applying/after-you-apply/making-right-decision), and [UCAS Extra](https://www.ucas.com/applying/after-you-apply/types-undergraduate-offers/extra-choices).

**Deadline behaviour.** UCAS distinguishes an early equal-consideration date for Oxford, Cambridge, and most medicine, dentistry, and veterinary courses; a main equal-consideration date for most undergraduate courses; a later boundary after which applications enter Clearing; and applicant-specific reply deadlines based on when decisions arrive. For 2027 entry, the published equal-consideration cutoffs are 15 October 2026 and 13 January 2027, both at 18:00 UK time, but these must be stored by cycle rather than coded as annual rules. See [UCAS dates and deadlines](https://www.ucas.com/applying/applying-to-university/dates-and-deadlines-for-uni-applications) and the explanation of [personal reply deadlines](https://www.ucas.com/events/2027-entry-if-you-receive-all-decisions-by-12-may-reply-by-2-june-477531).

**Checklist pattern.** Choose route and cycle → create Hub account → enter personal and education history → add choices → complete the personal-statement questions → arrange reference and predicted grades → pay and submit, or send to school for approval → track decisions → select firm/insurance → meet offer conditions.

**Calendar-safe events.** The relevant equal-consideration cutoff with UK timezone; the school's internal submission date; a clearly labelled planning target for a completed draft/reference; course-owned test, portfolio, interview, or audition dates; the personal reply deadline read from the applicant's account; Extra/Clearing windows where relevant. Never present an equal-consideration date as a universal final closure date.

### Canada — Ontario Universities' Application Centre (OUAC)

**Boundary and route.** OUAC is the centralized service for undergraduate applications to Ontario universities; universities, not OUAC, decide admission. Its current undergraduate application assigns applicants to Group A or Group B. Group A is tied to current Ontario high-school study, age, and OSSD conditions; international IB applicants will normally be Group B. Previous students may sometimes need to apply directly to an institution, and separate OUAC applications exist for professional categories. See the [OUAC undergraduate guide](https://www.ouac.on.ca/guide/undergrad-guide) and [OUAC's role](https://www.ouac.on.ca/guide/undergrad-about).

**Choices.** Students can apply to as many Ontario universities as they wish, but are normally limited to three programme choices at one university, with possible tighter university rules. Choice numbering usually does not affect admission, although an institution must disclose exceptions and can use ordering information for planning. See [OUAC: My Choices](https://www.ouac.on.ca/guide/undergrad-my-choices/).

**Deadline behaviour.** Group A has a common application deadline. Group B deadlines differ by university and programme. Transcripts, portfolios, auditions, interviews, and supplemental applications may each have their own date, and offer-response timing also differs by applicant group and offer. The 2027 guide, for example, lists 15 January 2027 for Group A but directs Group B applicants to institution dates. See [OUAC deadlines](https://www.ouac.on.ca/planning/deadlines/), [how to apply](https://www.ouac.on.ca/guide/undergrad-how-to-apply/), and [responding to an offer](https://www.ouac.on.ca/guide/undergrad-respond).

**Checklist pattern.** Confirm correct OUAC group/application → research university and programme codes → collect identity, education, and transcript information → create account → complete personal/background sections → add choices and programme options → arrange the right transcript route → review, declare, pay, and submit → save OUAC reference number → activate each university's follow-up portal → complete every university supplement/document task → respond to offers through the instructed route.

**Calendar-safe events.** The route-specific application deadline; programme and supplemental cutoffs; transcript/document dates; audition/interview appointments; expected acknowledgement check; conditional-offer requirements; and the actual response deadline contained in an offer. Do not export the Group A date for an international Group B student.

### Canada — ApplyAlberta

**Boundary and route.** ApplyAlberta serves undergraduate applications to Alberta's publicly funded post-secondary institutions. It provides identity/profile and transcript-transfer infrastructure, but after the student starts a new application they are directed to the chosen institution's portal to apply and pay. Institutions not in the partner list require a direct route. See [ApplyAlberta's overview](https://prod.applyalberta.ca/), [partner institutions](https://prod.applyalberta.ca/institutions/), and [how to apply](https://prod.applyalberta.ca/how-to-apply/).

**Choices and deadlines.** This is not one ranked provincial application. Each institution application is distinct, fees are paid to institutions, and deadlines belong to the institution/programme. ApplyAlberta transfers available Alberta high-school and participating post-secondary transcripts; records from outside Alberta, including an international IB record, may have to be ordered separately. See [ApplyAlberta: how to apply](https://prod.applyalberta.ca/how-to-apply/).

**Checklist pattern.** Confirm partner institution → obtain or create Alberta Student Number → create Alberta.ca account → complete ApplyAlberta profile and education history → start institution application → finish application and payment in institution portal → determine which transcripts ApplyAlberta can transfer → independently arrange outside-Alberta/IB evidence → monitor the institution account for further requirements.

**Calendar-safe events.** Institution application and priority dates; document/test/supplement deadlines; the date by which the student should verify transcript delivery; and offer-response or deposit dates from the institution. “Create profile” and “request documents” can be planning tasks, but are not official hard deadlines unless a source makes them so.

### Canada — EducationPlannerBC

**Boundary and route.** EducationPlannerBC supports applications to B.C.'s public post-secondary institutions through one account. Its application page currently lists 25 public institutions and identifies an external application path for an institution still migrating; private institutions are a separate category and may require direct application. See [EducationPlannerBC Apply](https://apply.educationplannerbc.ca/).

**Choices and deadlines.** A student may submit as many applications through the platform as desired. Some institutions allow two programmes on one application and others require another application. Each institution sets its own programme open/close dates and fees. Once submitted, EducationPlannerBC cannot edit the application; changes, documents, status, and next steps move to the institution. See the official [EducationPlannerBC help and application FAQ](https://www.educationplannerbc.ca/help?tab=0).

**Checklist pattern.** Check whether the institution uses EducationPlannerBC → create and confirm personal account → complete shared profile and academic history → select institution, programme, term, and any second choice → review and pay → verify on-screen, email, and submitted-application confirmation → order B.C. transcript or arrange international/IB evidence as directed → follow the institution's portal/email for documents and status.

**Calendar-safe events.** Institution/programme open and close dates; scholarship or priority dates; transcript and supplementary-material cutoffs; a post-submission task to confirm receipt; institution offer/deposit/registration dates. Do not invent a B.C.-wide application deadline.

### Denmark — Optagelse.dk and Coordinated Admission (KOT)

**Boundary and route.** Danish undergraduate applications are coordinated nationally through Optagelse.dk and assessed by institutions. Applicants normally do not submit a separate direct application, but institution-specific criteria, evidence, tests, and interviews remain essential. See [Study in Denmark: how to apply](https://studyindenmark.dk/study-options/how-to-apply).

**Choices and ranking.** Applicants can rank up to eight programmes. KOT makes at most one offer: the highest-ranked programme for which the applicant can receive a place. For each programme, the applicant must generate, hand-sign, and separately send a signature page to the relevant institution; uploading portal documents does not replace it. See [Study in Denmark: how to apply](https://studyindenmark.dk/study-options/how-to-apply).

**Applicant routes and deadlines.** International qualifications, including the IB whether earned inside or outside Denmark, use the 15 March, 12:00 CET route. That deadline also covers Quota 2 and admission-test programmes; convertible applications can also be considered in Quota 1. The 5 July, 12:00 CET route is for applicants with Danish upper-secondary education applying on GPA alone. Pending diplomas, priority changes, institution documents, written work, tests, and interviews create separate tasks. See [how to apply](https://studyindenmark.dk/study-options/how-to-apply) and [Quota 1 and Quota 2](https://studyindenmark.dk/study-options/how-to-apply/regulations-of-admission-quota-1-and-quota-2).

**Checklist pattern.** Confirm qualification route → verify general, specific, and language requirements → create applications → rank programmes → upload diploma/evidence → generate and separately send one signed page per programme → confirm local documentation and test/interview steps → update permitted priorities/materials before the relevant cutoff → receive coordinated decision.

**Calendar-safe events.** National route deadline; one signature-page task per programme; institution document cutoff; test/interview appointment; priority-change and pending-diploma cutoff; national decision date; standby availability period when selected. A submitted portal form must not be shown as “application complete” until the signature requirement is satisfied.

### Sweden — UniversityAdmissions.se

**Boundary and route.** UniversityAdmissions.se coordinates applications across Swedish universities and exposes the complete English-taught catalogue. International students are encouraged to use the first admissions round; the second is the regular Swedish round, has fewer English-taught options, and can be too late for a non-EU/EEA student to obtain a residence permit. Swedish applicants may instead use Antagning.se. See [admissions rounds](https://www.universityadmissions.se/en/key-dates-and-deadlines/admission-rounds-and-spring-semester-availability/) and the [bachelor's application overview](https://www.universityadmissions.se/en/apply-to-bachelors/).

**Choices and ranking.** A bachelor's applicant can rank up to eight selections. Ranking is binding in effect: offers can total no more than 45 credits per semester and lower-ranked selections that would exceed the maximum are deleted. See [ranking bachelor's selections](https://www.universityadmissions.se/en/apply-to-bachelors/rank-your-selections-bachelors/).

**Applicant routes and deadlines.** Every round has distinct application/ranking, application-fee or fee-exemption, supporting-document, result, and sometimes reply dates. For autumn 2027, for example, the international first round closes 15 January 2027 with documents/fee due 1 February, while the second round closes 15 April. Finishing IB/EB and some EU/EEA applicants in the second round have a later final-qualification date. Final-year bachelor applicants can be disadvantaged in the first round because selection occurs before final grades exist. See [autumn semester dates](https://www.universityadmissions.se/en/key-dates-and-deadlines/autumn-semester-dates/) and [final-year applicant guidance](https://www.universityadmissions.se/en/apply-to-bachelors/provide-application-documents-bachelors/students-in-their-final-year-of-upper-secondary-studies/).

**Checklist pattern.** Select appropriate round → create account → search, add, and rank up to eight choices → pay fee or document exemption → submit country- and IB-specific academic, English, identity, and programme materials → monitor missing-item status → receive selection result → reply if the selected round requires it → follow any university response instructions.

**Calendar-safe events.** Round opening; application/ranking cutoff; fee/exemption cutoff; supporting-document date; permitted final-IB-result date; result publication; applicant-specific or round-specific reply date. The product should actively warn when an IB result timeline conflicts with the selected admissions round.

### Ireland — Central Applications Office (CAO)

**Boundary and route.** CAO processes applications to listed undergraduate courses at participating Irish institutions, while institutions decide admission. Direct or separate routes can still exist for some international, part-time, advanced-entry, or other categories, so route selection must precede checklist generation. See the [CAO handbook page](https://www.cao.ie/handbook.php) and [2026 CAO Handbook](https://www2.cao.ie/handbook/handbook2026/hb.pdf).

**Choices and ranking.** CAO uses two independent preference lists: up to ten Level 8 courses and ten Level 7/6 courses. Choices must be placed in genuine preference order. An applicant can receive an offer from each list in one round but accept only one; accepting a lower-preference course does not block later consideration for higher preferences. See the official [CAO course-choice guide](https://www2.cao.ie/downloads/documents/course_choices_list.pdf) and [offer FAQ](https://www.cao.ie/?page=faq&type=offers).

**Deadline behaviour.** The cycle separates discounted registration, normal closing, restricted-course and special-category conditions, HEAR/DARE form and evidence dates, late application, Change of Mind, qualification evidence, offer rounds, and short offer-reply windows. The 2026 handbook, for example, lists normal closing at 17:00 on 1 February, late closing at 17:00 on 1 May, and Change of Mind closing at 17:00 on 1 July; dates must be refreshed per cycle. See [CAO dates and tips](https://www2.cao.ie/downloads/documents/2026/dates_and_tips_2026.pdf) and the [2026 handbook](https://www2.cao.ie/handbook/handbook2026/hb.pdf).

**Checklist pattern.** Confirm CAO route/category → create application number → enter all qualifications → build both lists in genuine order → identify restricted programmes → provide required results, translations, English evidence, and applicant-category evidence → complete HEAR/DARE or mature route if applicable → inspect Statement of Application Record → make permitted changes → monitor rounds → accept before the offer-specific reply time.

**Calendar-safe events.** Normal and applicable special-category deadlines; HEAR/DARE form and document cutoffs; late window; Change of Mind window; tests/interviews; application-record verification; confirmed offer releases; each personal acceptance deadline. Do not create a fixed event from an officially “TBC” offer date.

### Netherlands — Studielink

**Boundary and route.** Studielink is the official registration/application portal for Dutch higher education, but applicants must check the programme and institution first: institutional portals, foreign-applicant routes, documents, selection, and enrolment tasks may supplement Studielink. The Dutch government directs first-time higher-education applicants to register through Studielink while telling students to check programme requirements with the university or university of applied sciences. See [Government.nl application guidance](https://www.government.nl/themes/education/secondary-vocational-education-mbo-and-tertiary-higher-education/tertiary-higher-education/applying-for-a-course-at-an-institute-of-tertiary-education) and [Studielink](https://www.studielink.nl/).

**Choice and deadline behaviour.** There is no general national preference ranking comparable to Denmark or Sweden. Regular bachelor's registration generally has a 1 May deadline; fixed-intake (`numerus fixus`) programmes use 15 January and institution-run selection. Some programmes or institutions set different/earlier dates. An offered fixed-intake place creates a short personal acceptance window, so it must be treated as triggered rather than preloaded. See [Government.nl application guidance](https://www.government.nl/themes/education/secondary-vocational-education-mbo-and-tertiary-higher-education/tertiary-higher-education/applying-for-a-course-at-an-institute-of-tertiary-education) and [Study in NL: how to apply](https://www.studyinnl.org/plan-your-stay/how-to-apply).

**Checklist pattern.** Verify programme route and precise deadline → create Studielink access via DigiD or foreign-applicant login → add prior education → submit enrolment request → activate institution portal and upload evidence → complete selection tasks for fixed-intake study → review ranking/offer → accept within personal window → finish diploma, payment, immigration, and enrolment conditions.

**Calendar-safe events.** The exact programme deadline; 15 January only for applicable numerus-fixus choices; selection assignments/tests; ranking/decision publication; acceptance deadline calculated from actual offer; 1 May for applicable regular routes; institution enrolment and payment tasks. A Studielink request is not necessarily a completed institutional application.

### United States and other member destinations — Common App

**Boundary and route.** Common App is a platform used by more than 1,100 member colleges worldwide, not a national US admissions authority or universally exclusive route. Institutions choose whether and how to use it; Yale, for example, accepts Common App, Coalition, or QuestBridge, while Stanford currently directs first-year applicants to Common App. See [Common App: how it works](https://www.commonapp.org/apply/), [Yale's application options](https://admissions.yale.edu/apply), and [Stanford first-year application](https://admission.stanford.edu/apply/first-year/apply.html).

**Admission plans and restrictions.** Each college owns requirements and dates. Supported plan types include Regular Decision, Rolling Decision, Early Decision I/II, Early Action I/II, and Restrictive/Single-Choice Early Action. These are not just labels: Early Decision is binding and restrictive plans can limit other early applications. Common App does not support a first-year fall deadline earlier than 15 October and does not ask applicants to rank other schools, but institution policies must still be checked. See [Common App guiding principles](https://www.commonapp.org/about/guiding-principles) and the [application dictionary](https://www.commonapp.org/static/036088b1889f436e468a065c6a5933b7/Resource_FYTR_AppDictionary_ENG_2025.06.24_3.pdf).

**Requirements and deadlines.** The shared profile, activities, education, and main essay are only part of the work. Colleges can add questions, writing, testing, recommendations, portfolios, and financial-aid requirements. Counselors supply school reports and transcripts; teachers and other recommenders may have roles. Each college's page and Requirements Grid show current requirements and deadlines. See the [first-year application guide](https://www.commonapp.org/apply/first-year-students/). Common App's international guidance states that an application must be submitted by 23:59 in the applicant's local timezone, but separate materials or institution portals can follow other rules; store the source-specific timezone rather than assuming one for the whole plan. See [Common App for international applicants](https://www.commonapp.org/static/0b69c880faf13e59a0ce684754e48f60/FY_International_CAReady_2023.03.23_0.pdf).

**Checklist pattern.** Confirm accepted application platform → choose admission plan and check cross-application restrictions → create shared profile → add colleges → complete shared sections → complete each college supplement → invite counselor/recommenders and verify their requirements → arrange testing/transcripts/portfolio where required → submit each college separately and pay/waive fee → activate college status portals → complete financial-aid and missing-material tasks → respond by offer deadline.

**Calendar-safe events.** Each college-plan application cutoff; college supplement/material deadline; recommender request and school-internal planning dates; testing/portfolio/interview dates; financial-aid priority date; rolling-application opening and a student-chosen target (not a fabricated hard close); actual offer reply/deposit date. The product must block or warn about incompatible Early Decision/Restrictive Early Action combinations.

### Australia — Universities Admissions Centre (UAC, NSW and ACT)

**Boundary and route.** UAC handles undergraduate applications to participating institutions and courses in New South Wales and the Australian Capital Territory, including a dedicated international route. Institutions decide which courses participate in each offer round, and some courses or schemes have separate requirements. See [UAC undergraduate applications and offers](https://uac.edu.au/current-applicants/undergraduate-applications-and-offers) and [international applications and offers](https://uac.edu.au/current-applicants/international-applications-and-offers).

**Choices and ranking.** Most undergraduate applicants can select up to five preferences; international Year 12 applicants can select up to six. Order matters. In an offer round the applicant receives at most one offer, for the highest preference for which they are eligible and competitive; lower choices are not considered in that round. Preferences can be changed, but are locked for short periods around offer processing. See [selecting course preferences](https://uac.edu.au/future-applicants/how-to-apply-for-uni/selecting-your-course-preferences) and [undergraduate applications and offers](https://uac.edu.au/current-applicants/undergraduate-applications-and-offers).

**Deadline behaviour.** UAC publishes repeated offer rounds. Each can have a separate apply/pay deadline, PDF-document deadline, preference-change cutoff, and offer-release timestamp. There are also early-offer schemes, an early-bird fee date, main rounds after results, course-specific early closing, and later vacancy rounds. All published UAC times use Sydney time. UAC already exposes “Add to calendar” actions for key dates across Google, Apple, Outlook, and Office 365, which is a useful precedent for typed calendar export. See [UAC key dates](https://uac.edu.au/key-dates) and [undergraduate applications and offers](https://uac.edu.au/current-applicants/undergraduate-applications-and-offers).

**IB route.** UAC derives an IB Admissions Score for eligible diploma holders and publishes IB-result handling instructions; the application plan should connect result-release and document tasks to the relevant offer round. See [UAC guidance for IB Diploma applicants](https://uac.edu.au/future-applicants/admission-criteria/ib-applicants).

**Checklist pattern.** Confirm UAC/course route and applicant type → create application → enter qualifications and identifiers → add genuine-order preferences → apply/pay → upload required documents → complete any early-offer or equity scheme → lock preferences for target round → monitor results/score conversion → receive and accept offer → remove or reorder successful choice if seeking a later higher preference.

**Calendar-safe events.** The target round's apply/pay cutoff, document cutoff, preference-lock time, and offer release; early-bird fee and early-scheme dates; course-specific close; IB results/score date; actual offer acceptance deadline. Do not export every UAC round to every student—export only the rounds connected to saved choices and current application state.

## The application-plan model

The minimal hierarchy should be:

```text
Jurisdiction
  └─ Application system
       └─ Applicant route
            └─ Application cycle / intake
                 └─ Application (one or more choices)
                      ├─ Choice and rank
                      ├─ Requirement/checklist task
                      ├─ Deadline or availability window
                      ├─ Evidence/source
                      └─ State transition / triggered task
```

Do not attach `application_process` directly to `Institution`. The same institution can accept different portals, applicant categories, intakes, programmes, or admission plans. Conversely, one central application can contain choices across institutions.

### Suggested entities

- **ApplicationSystem** — name, operator, jurisdiction coverage, official URL, supported study levels, role (`central_decision`, `clearinghouse`, `shared_form`, `gateway`, `institution_portal`), and known exceptions.
- **ApplicantRoute** — conditions over qualification type, school location, citizenship/residency, age, current study, entry type, programme category, and intake; route-selection explanation; fallback/exception route.
- **ApplicationCycle** — intake/term, opens/closes, timezone, version, official source, last verified, and status.
- **Application** — student-owned plan, selected route, portal reference, state, fee state, and institutions/choices.
- **ApplicationChoice** — opportunity ID, portal code, rank where meaningful, rank semantics, admission plan, and choice status.
- **TaskDefinition** — reusable step with owner (`student`, `school`, `referee`, `institution`, `portal`, `testing_body`), prerequisites, completion evidence, and applicability rule.
- **StudentTask** — generated instance with status, assignee, notes, source, and date/window.
- **Deadline** — typed temporal rule described below; never just a bare date string.
- **ApplicationEvent** — submission, acknowledgement, document received, interview scheduled, offer received, result published, accepted, declined, or expired; events can generate new tasks and deadlines.

### Deadline contract

Every deadline or calendar candidate should carry at least:

```text
id
cycle_id / intake_id
scope: national | system | institution | programme | school | applicant
type: hard_cutoff | equal_consideration | priority | recommended_target |
      rolling_open | availability_window | document | result | offer_release |
      reply | deposit | interview | test | preference_lock
starts_at / due_at / ends_at
timezone (IANA name, not only an abbreviation)
all_day (boolean)
applicability_rule + plain-language explanation
source_url + source_owner
source_published_at / last_verified_at
verification: confirmed | provisional | tbc | superseded
consequence_of_missing
trigger_event_id (for a personalised relative deadline)
supersedes_deadline_id
```

The UI should always display the deadline's type and consequence. “13 January — equal consideration” is materially different from “13 January — application closes,” and “rolling” is a review policy, not a date.

## Walkthrough and checklist design

Build walkthroughs from reusable task definitions, but render them as a personal, ordered path:

1. **Confirm the route.** Ask only the facts that change process: qualification (IB), school/qualification country, citizenship/residency where relevant, current study, desired programme/intake, and applicant category.
2. **Explain the application container.** State whether choices share a form, whether ranking matters, maximum choices, fees, and whether submission hands off to an institution.
3. **Prepare people and evidence.** Separate student tasks from school, counselor, teacher, testing body, and institution tasks. Show dependencies such as “reference must be attached before submission.”
4. **Apply and verify.** Submission is followed by payment, reference/signature, receipt, and institution-portal activation checks where applicable.
5. **Complete selection steps.** Tests, interviews, portfolios, supplemental forms, document/result delivery, fee-exemption proof, or preference changes.
6. **Track decisions and conditions.** Offers can be conditional, ranked, round-based, or one of several independent results.
7. **Respond and enrol.** Create personal reply, deposit, final-result, immigration, housing, and registration tasks from the actual offer—not from a generic national template.

Each checklist item should show **why it applies**, **who owns it**, **what proves completion**, **the official source**, and **what happens if missed**. A student should be able to print one application plan without losing source URLs or timezone labels.

## Calendar interface and export policy

### Safe to export

- confirmed national/system dates that match the student's route and cycle;
- confirmed institution/programme application, document, portfolio, test, interview, preference-lock, and result dates;
- school/counselor internal dates when explicitly entered or supplied by the school;
- a personal reply, deposit, or enrolment deadline read from an offer/account;
- planning milestones created by the student, visibly labelled **personal target** rather than official deadline;
- availability windows as start/end events when a single due date would be misleading.

### Unsafe to export as hard deadlines

- last year's date projected into a new cycle;
- a Group A, domestic, or regular-route deadline applied to an international IB route;
- an “expected,” “usually,” “TBC,” or estimated offer date represented as confirmed;
- one university-wide deadline when the programme/intake has its own date;
- “rolling admission” converted into an invented closing day;
- a relative response window before the triggering offer exists;
- a portal submission date that omits a separate signature, reference, payment, or institution stage.

### Export behaviour

- Offer one-click `.ics` download plus Google/Apple/Outlook-compatible actions; UAC's official calendar interface demonstrates that cross-calendar export is understandable to applicants: [UAC key dates](https://uac.edu.au/key-dates).
- Use the source timezone in the event payload and show the student's local conversion in the UI. Preserve “18:00 UK time,” “12:00 CET,” “23:59 local time,” and “Sydney time” semantics rather than normalising them silently.
- Put the event type, affected choice(s), consequence, source link, and last-verified date in the description.
- Give every exported event a stable UID so corrections update rather than duplicate it. Keep a `SEQUENCE`/revision and cancellation path for superseded dates.
- Calendar subscriptions may update official dates; downloaded snapshots cannot. Say which mode the student is using and surface stale verification prominently.
- Default reminders should reflect risk without causing alarm: for example, several weeks plus several days for hard application/document cutoffs, and a shorter reminder for offer responses. Let students change them.
- A print view should group events by application and show timezone, owner, completion box, and source—not merely a monthly grid.

## Edge cases the design must handle

- An IB student is “international” for one system because of qualification location, but domestic for another because of citizenship or residence.
- The same institution/programme can have direct, common-platform, transfer, mature, professional, part-time, or returning-student routes.
- Final IB results may arrive after a first-round document deadline; Sweden explicitly makes route choice consequential for final-year applicants.
- A student can be academically eligible yet miss equal consideration, document completeness, fee proof, a signed page, selection activity, or a short offer reply.
- A rank can be informational (usually OUAC), irrelevant at initial submission (UCAS), or decisive (Denmark, Sweden, Ireland, UAC).
- An institution may accept multiple platforms, so “uses Common App” does not mean “Common App is the only route.”
- One portal account can produce multiple separately paid institution applications; one central application can contain many choices; one coordinated system can issue only one offer.
- Deadlines can be all-day, exact-time, local-to-applicant, source-timezone, or relative to another event.
- DST changes make abbreviations like CET, ET, and UK time unsafe as the only stored timezone. Store an IANA zone and retain the source wording.
- Offer and result dates can be revised, staggered, repeated in rounds, or unpublished until later.
- Tests, auditions, portfolios, scholarships, financial aid, visas, housing, and enrolment may live outside the admissions portal but still be critical to the plan.
- A school may need days or weeks between the student's internal hand-in and official submission.
- Minors may need parent/guardian involvement for fees, Early Decision agreements, consent, or travel/immigration steps.

## Adopt / avoid

### Adopt

- A route resolver that explains why it selected a process and lets a counselor correct it.
- A small, readable process card on each opportunity: **where to apply, route, choice/ranking effect, next confirmed deadline, and exceptions**.
- A generated “My application plan” with dependencies, task owners, completion evidence, and official sources.
- Typed deadlines with exact scope, timezone, consequence, cycle, and verification status.
- Triggered tasks after real events: acknowledgement → institution portal; offer → reply/deposit; final results → proof/condition check.
- A calendar/list/print triad over the same data, not three separate deadline stores.
- Prominent freshness and change notices, with version history for corrections.
- Neutral explanations of ranking and restrictions before the student rearranges choices or selects an early plan.

### Avoid

- A single “Apply” button that hides the route or sends every applicant to the same portal.
- A universal checklist copied to every university.
- Treating country as the only routing variable.
- Treating priority, equal consideration, rolling, final closure, document, and reply dates as synonyms.
- Predicting this year's cutoff from last year's date without an official confirmation.
- Conflating “submitted” with “complete.”
- Showing a previous admission score, points cutoff, or grade threshold as a guaranteed future result.
- Ranking schools visually in a way that conflicts with the applicant's actual portal preference order.
- Sending every possible national offer round to the student's calendar.
- Giving legal-sounding certainty when a route remains institution-specific or needs confirmation.

## Product implication

The site now has three complementary jobs:

1. **Discover possibilities** through the editorial atlas and expressive programme/institution stories.
2. **Decide responsibly** through requirements, personal fit, comparison, evidence, affordability, and preparation.
3. **Apply successfully** through route-aware walkthroughs, dependencies, verified deadlines, calendar export, and offer-to-enrolment follow-through.

The third job should feel calmer and more precise than the discovery experience without becoming a dry table. A selected destination can transition from the world view into a visual journey—“six steps from interest to submitted application”—while the underlying checklist and calendar remain exact, printable, accessible, and sourced.
