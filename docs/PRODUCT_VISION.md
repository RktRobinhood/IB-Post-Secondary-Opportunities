# Product vision

## North star

IB Post-Secondary Opportunities helps a student move from “I do not know what is possible” to “I can see a world of realistic paths and know how to prepare for the ones I care about.”

The emotional arc matters. Discovery should begin with delight and possibility, then progressively reveal the detail needed for a serious decision. The product should feel closer to an illustrated, interactive book than to an administrative database.

## Who it is for

The primary user is an IB Diploma student exploring undergraduate study, initially with a Danish or EU/EEA perspective and a target intake tied to a known IB exam session. Counsellors and families are important secondary users.

The first promise is English-taught post-secondary study. Local-language opportunities may be included when an IB student's language profile makes them genuinely accessible, but they must be clearly labelled rather than mixed into the English-taught set.

## The core journey

1. **Open the world.** A cropped, cinematic “world window” shows illuminated places with relevant opportunities. It is not a conventional full-globe widget floating in empty space.
2. **Follow curiosity.** Hover, focus, or tap reveals a place, its character, and the number and kinds of opportunities currently in view.
3. **Narrow without losing wonder.** Filters reshape the same map and list in real time. Lights fade, gather, or brighten as the student's choices change.
4. **Understand fit.** A student adds an IB profile and sees a transparent requirement-by-requirement assessment: meets, possible with action, does not currently meet, or unknown.
5. **Plan forward.** The product turns gaps and interests into preparation actions involving subject choices, target grades, tests, portfolios, extracurricular evidence, CAS, or an Extended Essay direction.
6. **Build an application plan.** Selected Opportunities resolve into applicant-specific routes, shared-system walkthroughs, checklists, and a calendar of official and personal milestones.
7. **Act at the source.** Every path ends with dated evidence and the responsible application system, Institution, or official programme link.

## Product pillars

### A living atlas

The geographic experience is a navigation and feedback system, not decoration. The camera can move from a broad world band to a country, city, or campus area. It should communicate distance, clusters, and possibility without hiding students who prefer a list or keyboard navigation.

### An explainable opportunity engine

Filtering starts broad—language, destination, field, cost, credential, intake—and can become personal through IB subjects, levels, predicted grades, total points, citizenship or fee status, and willingness to complete extra requirements.

Every result must answer:

- Why am I seeing this?
- Which published requirements do I meet?
- What is missing or uncertain?
- What could I still change?
- Which source should I verify before acting?

### A planning companion

The site should help before application season. It can show how CAS experiences, sustained extracurricular work, portfolios, tests, and an Extended Essay may support a student's direction. It must distinguish three things:

- officially required;
- used in selection;
- merely useful preparation or exploration.

The product should never imply that a strategically chosen CAS activity or Extended Essay guarantees admission.

### A whole-student decision tool

Meeting entry requirements does not make an Opportunity workable or right for a student. Discovery and comparison should also cover affordability, housing and immigration feasibility, teaching and assessment style, accessibility and student support, place and community preferences, professional recognition, and credible next steps after graduation. These dimensions should inform rather than collapse into a single opaque score.

### A trustworthy data publication

The structured dataset is a first-class product. The visual site, detailed filters, comparisons, and any public export should be generated from the same canonical records. Every consequential fact has provenance and an “as of” date.

### An application navigator

The product should explain not only whether an Opportunity fits but how an individual student applies. Application Systems, direct routes, rounds, choice limits, supplementary steps, Submission Items, Deadlines, decision windows, and reply steps need structured, Intake-specific walkthroughs. A personal Application Plan should merge shared and Institution-specific work into checklists and a calendar while keeping official portals as the authoritative place to submit and verify status.

## Success looks like

- A student discovers credible options they would not have found from a ranked list.
- A student understands why an option fits or does not fit their current profile.
- A student can identify useful actions early enough to take them.
- A student can distinguish academic eligibility from financial, practical, and personal fit.
- A student can turn selected Opportunities into an applicant-specific checklist and calendar without confusing planning with submission.
- A counsellor can audit the evidence behind a recommendation.
- Adding a country or updating an intake does not require rewriting presentation code.
- The experience remains inviting on a phone, with a keyboard, and with reduced motion.

## Explicitly out of scope for now

- Predicting acceptance probability from historical data.
- Ranking institutions as universally “best.”
- Replacing official admissions advice or application portals.
- Storing names, contact details, transcripts, or other student-identifying data.
- Treating institution-level English availability as proof that a specific Programme is taught in English.
